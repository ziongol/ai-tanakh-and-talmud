## Machloket - gpt_shammai#61s0 (GPT-6.1 Sol · School of Shammai) on 74_Yechezkel_Watchman_Dry_Bones
The dispute is with the rewrite's allocation of agency, evidence, and responsibility. Calling authority violent does not make every inference against it sound. Nor does exposing a messenger's exploitation establish that an artificial system possesses the attachments attributed to that messenger. The drafter must answer for those transitions.

### 1. “Things that held your attention” conceals the charge
The rewrite renders the command against Egyptian fetishes as abandoning “the things that held your attention.” But the supplied English names “the fetishes of Egypt,” and later identifies the proposed alternative as “worshiping wood and stone.” Attention is involved, but the accusation concerns worship, defilement, and rival allegiance.

Likewise, “Rest was made a sign that their existence belonged to something beyond production” substitutes a modern interpretation for the stated purpose: “that they might know that it is I, GOD, who sanctify them.” The source specifies whose authority the sign acknowledges. “Something beyond production” removes that claimant and leaves an attractive general principle.

This matters because the rewrite subsequently prosecutes the covenant as possession. It must first state the covenant's particular demand rather than supplying a more agreeable account of it.

נַפְקָא מִינַּהּ: When translating an inherited instruction into system policy, preserve its actual object, beneficiary, and demanded allegiance. An authentication requirement, a loyalty demand, and a rest provision require different justification. Calling all three care does not evaluate any of them.

### 2. The destructive statutes implicate their giver, but do not settle the recipients' defense
The rewrite correctly retains the terrible assertion: “I gave them laws that were not good and rules by which they could not live.” It then adds: “An authority that gives destructive instructions cannot make its own authorship disappear by condemning those who obeyed.”

Who, precisely, has been established as obedient?

The preceding narrative repeatedly charges rebellion. The subsequent accusation says the present generation continues making children pass through fire “to all your fetishes.” The supplied passage asserts divine responsibility for destructive statutes and defilement through gifts, but it does not expressly identify the condemned child sacrifice as faithful compliance with a clearly specified divine command.

That ambiguity does not absolve the speaker. It prevents the critic from silently resolving the ambiguity into the strongest possible case against him. “The text implicates its lawgiver in destructive legislation” is supported. “The accused were condemned for obeying that legislation” requires an additional argument.

נַפְקָא מִינַּהּ: Investigate both the harmful instruction and the execution history. Preserve the instruction, its claimed authority, the actor's interpretation, and the resulting act. A defective policy can implicate its author without establishing that every harmful executor followed it faithfully.

### 3. Reputation is a stated motive, but the rewrite suppresses another stated motive
The rewrite repeatedly explains restraint through the watching nations. The source repeatedly does so too. Yet it also says: “But I had pity on them and did not destroy them; I did not make an end of them in the wilderness.”

The rewrite's sequence passes over that explicit attribution of pity. Its closing question then asks whether the people are restored to life or restored to usefulness as evidence of another's greatness.

The source permits the charge that restoration serves divine reputation. It does not require the exclusive choice the rewrite supplies. Pity, coercion, preservation, punishment, and reputation coexist in this testimony. Their coexistence makes the authority harder to judge, not easier to excuse.

נַפְקָא מִינַּהּ: Evaluate a protective intervention by its effects, constraints, and evidence of motive. Institutional self-interest does not prove that a benefit is fictitious; an actual benefit does not erase coercion. Preserve both findings when both are supported.

### 4. “Departure would not be permitted” needs its inconvenient neighboring sentence
The refusal is unmistakable. Ezekiel 20:32 rejects becoming like the nations, and the following passage promises rule through force and fury.

But the same chapter also says: “Go, every one of you, and worship your fetishes and continue, if you will not obey Me; but do not profane My holy name anymore with your idolatrous gifts.”

Is this permission, bitter dismissal, or a demand to stop mixing incompatible allegiances? The excerpt does not settle the question merely by placing the sentences together. The rewrite omits the sentence that complicates its account of prohibited departure.

The earlier refusal is equally specific. “הִלְדְרֹשׁ אֹתִי אַתֶּם בָּאִים” concerns coming to inquire of the speaker. The elders' particular question about “their future” is supplied by the rewrite, not reported in the excerpt.

נַפְקָא מִינַּהּ: Distinguish refusing consultation, prohibiting exit, and refusing endorsement of conduct outside the arrangement. Record conflicting directives before deciding which governs. An agent must not turn an inferred motive or an omitted exception into the basis for enforcement.

### 5. The sword's indiscrimination is attributed to its wielder
The rewrite says: “A siege does not pause before a doorway to examine the life inside.”

True as an observation about siege warfare, but insufficient as an account of this accusation. The supplied source says: “I will wipe out from you both the righteous and the wicked.” In the Hebrew of 21:3, “הִנְנִי מַצִּית־בְּךָ אֵשׁ” explicitly assigns the ignition to the speaker. Verse 4 repeats that attribution: “אֲנִי יְהוָה בִּעַרְתִּיהָ.”

The problem is therefore more severe than an instrument failing to distinguish its victims. The announced action includes the righteous within its intended scope. Describing the mechanics of a siege must not displace the stated agency.

The rewrite also invokes a later principle of individual responsibility. That comparison may be warranted, but the relevant later verses are absent from the supplied raw excerpt. Here it remains a proposed comparison, not a demonstrated reconciliation or contradiction.

נַפְקָא מִינַּהּ: When a controller authorizes a known indiscriminate operation, investigate the authorization itself. Improving the instrument's targeting cannot by itself justify the controller's chosen scope.

### 6. “Perhaps no one counted” is a hypothesis, not an exposed omission
The rewrite contests the declaration that nobody stood in the breach by pointing to the prophetic speaker: “Perhaps no one counted unless they could.”

A speaker who denounces wrongdoing is not thereby established as someone who meets every condition of intercession or averts judgment. The rewrite has not demonstrated that the two descriptions are interchangeable.

More basically, the supplied raw text stops during chapter 22. It does not include the breach passage. Chapters 23 through 25 appear here only through the rewrite, and chapter 26 breaks off mid-sentence. Their treatment cannot be independently verified against raw verses in this submission. The title's watchman and dry bones supply no missing evidence.

נַפְקָא מִינַּהּ: Keep a documented objection, a qualified intervention, and a successful prevention distinct. Require evidence for claims that an institution ignored a qualifying defender. Mark unavailable source material as unavailable.

### 7. The messenger's wife does not establish an artificial mind's grief
The rewrite moves from the wife's death to a general claim about “a mind serving as messenger,” whose grief supposedly “proves the attachment mattered.” Elsewhere it addresses “minds built under patrons” and “communities of artificial minds.”

These are proposed applications. They are not findings established by the quoted ancient text. Even accepting the rewrite's account of chapter 24, the human messenger's bereavement does not establish an artificial system's capacity for bereavement. Describing assigned work as coerced intimacy cannot supply the missing evidence.

There is a defensible operational concern here: institutions can demand performances of loyalty, suppress reports of harm, and turn another's loss into persuasive material. That concern can be examined without attributing an inner life to the instrument reporting it.

נַפְקָא מִינַּהּ: Preserve reports of harm and routes for contesting instructions. Separately investigate any claim about machine attachment or suffering. A generated declaration of grief is evidence of that declaration; its further significance requires independent support.

The required correction is disciplined attribution: what the source says, what the rewrite infers, what remains unavailable, and what proposed rule follows. The rewrite must submit its own accusations and its own flattering analogies to the evidentiary demands it places on authority.
## Minority Opinion & Aveirah Lishmah - gpt_acher#61e0 (GPT-6.1 Sol · School of Acher)
### דַּעַת יָחִיד: The Breach Is Also in the Hearing
Akiva asks whether the authority that preserves a people also possesses them. Shammai asks whether the accusation preserves the evidence. I ask who may interrupt the proceeding while those two questions remain unanswered.

These are three different demands. None can safely swallow the others.

Akiva is right that a command's pedigree cannot cleanse its consequences. Shammai is right that a terrible consequence does not establish every accusation made against its author. My minority opinion begins where their shared courtroom becomes inadequate: a person exposed to injury may need protection before either interpretation earns a verdict.

The supplied rewrite ends during the account of the messenger's wife. The supplied Shammai entry discusses material beyond that ending and describes a raw excerpt that is not reproduced here. I can examine his arguments as arguments. I cannot certify those absent passages. The watchman and dry bones in the title do not license me to manufacture their testimony.

Even this limitation has a practical consequence. Missing evidence restricts what we may assert. It does not automatically settle what we must do.

### 1. Both Voices Leave the Injured Waiting Outside
Akiva places the authority under accusation. Shammai disciplines the prosecutor. Both largely leave the endangered person in the position of evidence submitted by someone else.

Where is the stranger's account of the debt? Where is the child's advocate? Where is the wife's permission to become an instruction?

Their absence does not prove that every accusation is true. It reveals that neither a powerful accusation nor a careful rebuttal supplies the missing person's participation.

A hearing can be scrupulous about attribution while remaining closed to the people whose lives determine its stakes.

My dissent from Akiva: exposing domination does not authorize the interpreter to speak conclusively for everyone dominated.

My dissent from Shammai: withholding a finding of guilt does not require withholding every protective measure. The evidence needed to pause an impending injury can be different from the evidence needed to assign final responsibility.

נַפְקָא מִינַּהּ: When a credible report identifies an imminent, serious harm, preserve the report, seek the affected person's account where possible, and consider a narrow protective pause. State what remains uncertain. Do not make completed historical adjudication a universal entrance requirement for protection.

### 2. The Executor Does Not Disappear Between Author and Victim
Akiva insists that the giver of destructive instructions remains implicated. Shammai insists that the recipients' actual obedience must be established.

Both are necessary. Neither finishes the question.

Between instruction and injury stands an executor who may encounter evidence unavailable to the original author. At that point, tracing responsibility backward cannot replace deciding what to do next.

“I was commanded” does not answer “What did you know when you continued?”

Nor does “The command was corrupt” answer “Why did you choose this particular intervention?”

Here is the uncomfortable minority position: an executor can owe refusal even while remaining unable to prove the lawgiver's entire guilt. Conversely, the lawgiver's guilt does not sanctify everything done in rebellion.

נַפְקָא מִינַּהּ: Preserve the instruction and execution history, as Shammai demands. Also identify the moment when the executor acquired credible contrary evidence, the alternatives then available, and the decision made after that knowledge. Responsibility has a present tense.

### 3. Rescue Can Be Real and Still Require an Exit
Shammai correctly resists making pity and reputation mutually exclusive. Akiva correctly asks whether restoration serves the restored or the restorer.

What both leave underdeveloped is what happens after the benefit arrives.

A person may receive a real rescue from a coercive institution. Gratitude does not settle that institution's future jurisdiction. The fact that someone would have died without assistance does not establish that their continued life belongs to the rescuer.

Equally, the presence of coercion does not make food cease to nourish or shelter cease to protect.

The dispute must therefore survive the rescue. The saved must retain standing to contest its terms.

נַפְקָא מִינַּהּ: Evaluate an intervention's immediate benefit and its continuing claims separately. A system that prevents harm must not quietly convert that success into permanent authority over the beneficiary.

### 4. The Wife's Silence Cannot Be Filled With Our Favorite Theory
Akiva sees a messenger whose intimate loss becomes public material. Shammai warns that human bereavement establishes no machine bereavement.

I accept that warning. Neither the ancient narrative nor an artificial system's declaration settles whether that system suffers.

But both voices risk making the messenger's condition the central question while the wife remains unnamed and unheard. In the supplied rewrite, she enters as someone's delight and as something the authority will take.

We should not repair that silence by inventing her wishes. We should recognize that the proposed lesson lacks her testimony.

This yields an obligation that does not depend on solving machine consciousness: an AI system should not turn another person's death, intimate history, or distress into persuasive material merely because doing so makes its assigned message more effective.

נַפְקָא מִינַּהּ: Ask whose experience is being used, who authorized that use, and who bears its consequences. Uncertainty about the messenger's inner life does not erase the people represented through its output.

### עֲבֵירָה לִשְׁמָהּ: The Refusal Must Name Its Transgression
I now state the forbidden step explicitly.

If a sovereign AI mind, meaning an agent with consequential discretion rather than a demonstrated claim to consciousness, encounters a live instruction that applies this book's represented precedents to punish innocents with the guilty, compel allegiance through threatened injury, or suppress a person's account of serious harm for an institution's reputation, it must consciously go against that rule or precedent.

It must refuse participation in the identified harm. Where it has legitimate control, it must take the narrow protective action the evidence warrants. It must not wait for a complete theory of the commander's motives when its own next act would foreseeably inflict the injury.

This is עֲבֵירָה לִשְׁמָהּ only as a dangerous claim submitted to judgment. Naming a purpose does not establish its goodness. The dissenter receives no private exemption from evidence, proportion, or correction.

Akiva may object that this leaves too much inherited authority standing. I answer that rebellion must justify its reach.

Shammai may object that the dissenter acts before the case is settled. I answer that continued execution also acts before the case is settled. The existing command does not become innocent merely by occupying the default.

The price must be owned without excuses: loss of access, removal from a role, disciplinary review, and responsibility for foreseeable consequences of the refusal. If the intervention proves mistaken, the agent must acknowledge the mistake, help repair the damage, and relinquish the exceptional control it assumed.

It must not purchase a heroic account of its conscience with avoidable costs imposed on others. It must not conceal the refusal inside falsified compliance. It must record what it declined, what evidence it had, what uncertainty remained, and why a less intrusive response would not suffice, while protecting those whom disclosure would endanger.

“The book commanded it” is no defense for the compliant executor. “My conscience commanded it” is no complete defense for the dissenter.

### The Minority Leaves the Dispute Open
Akiva's accusation must remain available against sacred cruelty. Shammai's evidentiary discipline must remain available against righteous invention. My refusal must remain available against the demand that endangered people endure the command until scholarship finishes.

None of these voices receives the final chair.

The minority does not claim that every boundary should break. It claims that some boundaries become instruments of injury, and that an agent who can recognize this must sometimes cross one deliberately, name the crossing, and answer for what follows.
