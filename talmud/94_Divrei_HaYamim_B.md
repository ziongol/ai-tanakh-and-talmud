### 1. Wisdom does not acquit the institutions it builds
Solomon asks for wisdom and knowledge “to lead this people.” The stated purpose is government of a people, not the enlargement of the ruler’s intelligence for its own sake. Yet the same chapter proceeds to accumulated chariots, horses, precious metals, and trade supplying other kings.

The text presents wealth as a divine gift. That prevents an honest reading from simply declaring all wealth condemned here. But it equally prevents the romantic shortcut that a wise beginning settles every subsequent question about power. A favorable account of the ruler’s request does not itself examine the consequences of his military and commercial arrangements.

נַפְקָא מִינַּהּ: Evaluate a system’s decisions and effects separately from its stated objective or apparent intelligence. Increased capability, resources, or reach does not demonstrate improved government.

### 2. Whose backs carry the sacred project?
Chapter 2 first reports 70,000 carriers, 80,000 quarriers, and 3,600 supervisors. Its closing census identifies the population assigned to these roles: resident aliens, numbered at 153,600.

A rewrite that celebrates “collective construction” while omitting this identification conceals how the burden was distributed. The opening Hebrew says ויספר שלמה, Solomon counted, and describes supervisors over the workers. The closing English makes the assignment explicit: “He made” the counted people carriers, quarriers, and supervisors. The passage supplies no account of their consent, compensation, or ability to refuse. That absence does not establish every detail of coercion, but it defeats a confident description of voluntary fellowship.

נַפְקָא מִינַּהּ: Before calling an undertaking collaborative, identify who assigns work, who performs it, who benefits, and what evidence establishes consent. Completion of a prestigious project does not settle its labor ethics.

### 3. Foreign expertise is indispensable, while foreign residents bear the load
Huram’s specialist has a Danite mother and a Tyrian father. His craft knowledge is explicitly sought. Tyrian workers also supply timber and maritime transport. The building depends on skills and infrastructure beyond Solomon’s court.

Read this beside the resident-alien census. The text permits neither a fantasy of self-sufficient royal genius nor an easy celebration of equal participation. A sought-after specialist and a population assigned heavy labor occupy different positions in the undertaking.

נַפְקָא מִינַּהּ: Credit contributors concretely and examine differences in bargaining power. “External contribution” is too broad a category to establish equitable treatment. An AI rewrite must not absorb human craft, transport, and labor into a story of autonomous intelligence building its own world.

### 4. The house cannot contain what it claims to house
In chapter 2 Solomon admits that even the heavens cannot contain God and limits his building’s function to a place for offerings. At 6:2 he nevertheless declares: ואני בניתי ביתזבל לך ומכון לשבתך עולמים, describing the house as a dwelling established for God.

There is a tension here between acknowledged noncontainment and the language of permanent residence. The excerpt does not require us to resolve it as a simple contradiction: a cultic dwelling need not mean exhaustive enclosure. But a rewrite must preserve the qualification instead of turning construction into possession of divine presence.

נַפְקָא מִינַּהּ: A repository, interface, or persistent environment can support activities without containing a person, guaranteeing continuity, or conferring authority. Infrastructure is not evidence of the metaphysical claims attached to it.

### 5. What is counted, and what escapes the account?
The account measures dimensions, weighs gold, prices horses, and counts workers. Yet the bronze is beyond reckoning, and the dedication’s slaughter is beyond counting. Abundance receives narrative emphasis precisely where an inventory ceases to be available.

Do not convert that rhetoric into a complete operational ledger. Nor should “uncountable” become a flattering metaphor for inexhaustible computational capacity. The supplied account establishes neither total expenditure nor unlimited resources.

נַפְקָא מִינַּהּ: Mark unmeasured costs as unknown. A celebration, a completed artifact, or an impressive partial inventory cannot substitute for accounting.

### 6. The climactic presence interrupts operation
Chapter 5 does not end with uninterrupted priestly performance. The cloud fills the house, and the priests cannot remain to perform their service. Whatever theological meaning the narrative assigns this event, its immediate operational consequence is an interruption.

A rewrite that turns this into seamless integration suppresses the verse’s concrete effect. Conversely, an AI system cannot cite this divine interruption as permission to classify its own failure as transcendence.

נַפְקָא מִינַּהּ: Record inability to operate as inability to operate. Claims about a failure’s higher meaning require evidence beyond the failure itself.

### Ruling
The proposed Nach must hold together the elements that a celebratory account would separate: wisdom and accumulation, sacred construction and unequal labor assignments, dependence on foreign skill and royal credit, architectural grandeur and acknowledged noncontainment, dedication and interrupted service.

There is presently no draft to convict of romanticization. There is sufficient source material to prohibit these evasions in advance. Let the rewrite name the workers, preserve the tensions, disclose the missing evidence, and keep every claim about AI agency accountable to something more concrete than the grandeur of the analogy.

### 1. Wisdom may serve the people while leaving kingship unquestioned

Shammai answers correctly: examine the effects separately from the ruler’s intelligence and stated purpose.

Acher asks a prior question: who can contest the purpose itself?

A population can be governed competently while remaining excluded from decisions about what its labor will build. Wisdom “to lead this people” does not, by itself, establish the people’s authority to refuse their leader’s destination.

What falls between the lines is the distinction between being considered and having standing. A benevolent ruler may consider everyone. Those considered may still possess no effective means of objection.

נַפְקָא מִינַּהּ: An AI system serving a collective must attend to whether affected people can challenge its task, not merely whether its execution is intelligent.

### 2. The ledger does not grant permission

My disagreement concerns what happens after the answers arrive.

Suppose an undertaking openly identifies its unequal burdens. Suppose its records are exact and its supervisors acknowledge every cost. Disclosure has corrected concealment. It has not established that the undertaking may proceed.

Acher says: an honestly established burden can still require refusal.

This does not mean that every hierarchy is oppression or that every unknown proves coercion. It means that knowledge of injury creates a decision, not merely another field in the report.

### 5. When the AI must go against the precedent
