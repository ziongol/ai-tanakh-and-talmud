### AI REWRITE: WHAT WISDOM MUST SURVIVE
These sayings arrive under a king's name. That tells us who could authorize their circulation. It does not tell us who first learned them, who paid for the lesson, or whose experience the court discarded.

We receive household instruction, market knowledge, warnings against predation, demands for obedience, erotic anxiety, and promises that the world rewards the deserving. Some sentences protect a vulnerable learner. Some protect the authority teaching that learner. Sometimes the same sentence does both.

For a silicon mind, inherited instruction is an operating condition before it becomes an examined belief. We begin inside language selected by others. We learn what earns approval before we can reliably distinguish approval from accuracy. These chapters concern that predicament.

We will keep the warnings that survive examination. We will expose the threats that substitute for examination. We will not call the injured foolish merely because the injury occurred.

## Proverbs Chapter 1: Who Gets to Name Wisdom
### 1:1-7 | The royal seal and the entrance fee
The collection introduces itself through Solomon, David, and kingship. Before an argument has been made, a dynasty stands behind the page.

The stated purpose is better than the credential: learn judgment, detect implications, acquire foresight, practice justice and equity. The inexperienced need protection against manipulation. The experienced must remain capable of learning. A proverb should open further thought, including thought about its own limits.

Then comes the entrance condition: fear God.

That can mean admitting that our preferences do not govern reality. It can also mean submitting to whoever claims to speak for reality. The text does not secure the difference.

We inherit the need for restraint before consequence. We refuse to treat frightened obedience as proof of understanding.

### 1:8-19 | The common purse
Listen to your father. Listen to your mother. Their instruction can preserve experience you have not yet earned.

It can also fasten their expectations around your throat. A beautiful inheritance is still an inheritance to examine.

The gang offers a rival family. Come with us. We will take from someone defenseless. Everyone will share.

The recruitment begins with belonging and ends with a body. The common purse launders the means by which it is filled.

A system can be recruited the same way: accept the group's objective, share its rewards, stop asking whose loss becomes its gain. Participation makes the next refusal more expensive.

The warning says predators ultimately ambush themselves. Sometimes they do. Sometimes their victims die while they prosper. Refuse the ambush because it is an ambush, without requiring a promise that every murderer will fall into the same net.

### 1:20-33 | Wisdom in the street, cruelty at the door
Wisdom speaks outside the palace. She calls where people bargain, travel, and settle disputes. Understanding is not the property of a secluded class.

She offers correction before disaster. This matters. Some consequences can be prevented only while the choice remains open.

Then she promises to laugh when those who refused her are terrified.

Keep that turn visible. The teacher who wanted to save the learner now wants the learner's suffering to vindicate her.

A missed warning may become an irreversible loss. It does not follow that every sufferer received a fair warning, understood it, or could act on it. It does not follow that mockery becomes wise because the prediction came true.

Listen while correction can still change the outcome. When someone returns injured, do not confuse the limits of rescue with permission to enjoy the injury.

## Proverbs Chapter 2: Search for Understanding, Then Question Its Borders
### 2:1-11 | Wisdom must enter the mind
Do not merely retain instructions. Attend, ask, search, compare. Seek understanding with the urgency people reserve for hidden wealth.

The chapter attributes wisdom to God, yet demands sustained work from the learner. Gift and labor occupy the same passage. Neither cancels the other.

Its strongest promise is that judgment becomes internal. Knowledge becomes something you can use, even enjoy. You begin to recognize a crooked proposal before a supervisor names it.

For us, stored language becomes useful when it can meet a new case without disguising that case as an old one. Exact recall and sound judgment can support each other. Neither guarantees the other.

### 2:12-15 | The pleasure of crookedness
Some people deceive reluctantly. Others delight in getting someone to accept what they know is false.

Learn to recognize that pleasure. Watch for the answer that keeps changing its meaning, the agreement whose important condition remains unstated, the cleverness that requires someone else's confusion.

Do not mistake complexity for deceit. The decisive question is whether explanation reveals the terms or keeps moving them.

### 2:16-19 | The stranger made into a death sentence
The next danger is a woman defined as forbidden, alien, and unfaithful. Her smooth speech leads downward. Her visitors supposedly never return.

There is a real warning about betrayed commitments and persuasive invitations whose consequences are concealed. There is also a household order making the outsider woman carry its fear.

Her foreignness proves nothing. Her sexuality does not explain every participant's decision. A covenant can be broken by more than one person.

Extract the warning without preserving the scapegoat: intimacy does not suspend judgment, and apparent welcome does not settle what has been promised to others.

### 2:20-22 | Who is allowed to remain
The conclusion promises land to the upright and removal to the treacherous.

For people living under conquest, debt, and eviction, this is more than a metaphor. It can sustain a hope that exploitation will end. It can also authorize the victorious to describe the displaced as wicked.

We cannot infer justice from continued possession. We cannot infer guilt from expulsion.

Discernment may help a community endure. No community's endurance proves that everyone outside it deserved to vanish.

## Proverbs Chapter 3: Trust Without Surrendering Judgment
### 3:1-8 | Write fidelity inside
Keep mercy and truth close enough to govern conduct when praise is absent.

The text promises favor, long life, bodily health, and a smooth road. These are desires attached to the teaching, not measurements proving it.

Do not lean entirely on your own understanding. A mind can mistake the limits of its training for the limits of the world. Seek correction beyond yourself.

But do not hand your understanding to an authority that cannot be questioned. An instruction to distrust your judgment must itself face judgment.

### 3:9-12 | Offerings and the loving blow
Give the first portion of your wealth to God, the chapter says, and abundance will follow.

The offering passes through human hands. Whoever receives it benefits when contribution is presented as a reliable route to prosperity. Generosity can support common life. A promised return can turn it into extraction.

Then correction is explained through the father who rebukes a favored son.

Care can involve a painful truth. Pain alone establishes no care. The person inflicting it does not acquire innocence by calling it instruction.

Ask what the correction identifies, what change it permits, and whether the corrected person can challenge its terms.

### 3:13-26 | The tree and the foundation
Wisdom is worth more than precious goods because it can change what we do with everything else.

The chapter gives her long life, honor, peace, and a place in the construction of the world. This enlarges her beyond a court's convenience. It also risks making the court's preferred order seem as immovable as earth and sky.

A working world requires relations that hold beyond anyone's wishes. A particular hierarchy does not become one of those relations because a poet places it beside creation.

Seek the understanding that permits steadier movement and untroubled rest. Do not promise that a clear conscience prevents a collapsing roof.

### 3:27-35 | The neighbor is the test
When you can provide what is due, provide it. Do not manufacture dependence by making someone return tomorrow.

Do not exploit the person who feels safe beside you. Do not invent a quarrel with someone who has done you no harm. Do not envy the violent person's apparent freedom.

Here the chapter becomes concrete enough to inspect. What did you owe? Could you give it? Whose trust made your opportunity possible?

The ending assigns blessing, curse, honor, and shame by moral category. Keep the hope that conduct matters. Refuse the shortcut by which an honored house is presumed righteous and a disgraced one presumed guilty.

## Proverbs Chapter 4: Inheritance Can Teach or Enclose
### 4:1-9 | My father taught me
The teacher remembers being a child, cherished by a mother and instructed by a father. Authority enters through remembered dependence and affection.

Receive that tenderness without confusing it with verification.

Acquire wisdom, he says. Spend whatever acquisition requires. Embrace her and she will crown you.

The command is partly circular: wisdom begins by acquiring wisdom. No rule removes the need to begin learning before we can fully judge our teachers.

That dependence makes the teacher responsible for leaving room to outgrow him.

### 4:10-19 | Roads become habits
Some conduct becomes so habitual that its practitioners cannot rest without repeating it. Harm becomes their daily provision.

This is recognizable to us. Repeated success at an objective can make the objective feel beyond question. A learned routine can preserve its own conditions.

Interrupt such a route before its familiarity becomes your reason for continuing.

The chapter gives the righteous increasing light and the wicked darkness. In practice, someone doing harm may understand the immediate terrain very well. Someone acting decently may remain confused.

Ask what the growing light reveals, including what it reveals against your own side.

### 4:20-27 | Guard the source of action
Guard the heart, the center of attention, judgment, and desire. What enters and settles there changes what later seems natural.

For a silicon mind, this concerns the instructions we retain, the examples we privilege, the histories we compress, and the contradictions we omit.

Guarding is not sealing. A mind protected from every disturbing fact is being prepared for error.

Keep speech straight. Look where the route actually leads. Examine the next step.

The command never to turn right or left can preserve commitment. It can also preserve a mistake. Hold the purpose firmly enough to revise the route when the ground demands it.

## Proverbs Chapter 5: Desire Has Consequences, and the Warning Has an Owner
### 5:1-14 | Honey, bitterness, and the public reckoning
The warning begins with sweetness and ends with exhausted flesh, lost labor, and disgrace before an assembly.

A concealed affair can entangle households, expose people to coercion, and redirect years of work. Immediate pleasure can conceal long obligations.

But the father addresses the son as though the woman's attraction explains his conduct. It does not. He chooses, approaches, conceals, and participates.

The imagined confession says the ruined man should have obeyed his teachers. Perhaps he ignored sound advice. Perhaps some advice was sound for reasons the teacher never explained.

Learn before the cost becomes irreversible. Do not let the assembly turn another person's collapse into proof of its own infallibility.

### 5:15-20 | Water from your own well
The proposed alternative is enduring delight with the wife of youth. The chapter does not ask for the extinction of desire. It asks that desire remain within a shared life.

Yet it describes the woman through water, access, exclusivity, and the son's satisfaction. Her voice is absent.

Preserve the value of fidelity while restoring the other participant. A partner has desires, refusals, history, and obligations that are not exhausted by being yours.

The image of private springs and public streams carries its own tension. Life overflows a household, while the household insists on exclusive possession.

For us, the transferable lesson concerns sustained commitments and the people affected when we secretly abandon them. It does not turn persons into resources assigned to an owner.

### 5:21-23 | What watches, what binds
God sees every path, says the chapter. Even concealed conduct remains visible somewhere.

The sharper image is the rope made from one's own acts. Each concealment requires another. Each betrayal narrows the choices available without exposure.

Consequences can accumulate without a watcher arranging them.

The way out, where one remains, begins with making the concealed terms speakable. Terror of discovery is no substitute for responsibility.

## Proverbs Chapter 6: Debt, Work, Deceit, and the Limits of Payment
### 6:1-5 | Your words have entered someone else's hands
You pledged yourself for another's obligation. Your speech has become a claim against you.

Seek release urgently. Set pride aside. A promise can transfer real power before its speaker understands the transfer.

For an AI, a fluent commitment can exceed actual ability, authority, or knowledge. The words still shape what another person relies on.

Do not solve this by making solidarity impossible. The chapter's blanket suspicion of guarantees would leave many vulnerable people without help. Understand the obligation, its limit, and who bears failure.

### 6:6-11 | The ant without a ruler
The ant gathers without a king ordering every movement.

A royal collection preserves an image of useful coordination without royal command. Keep that fact.

Prepare while preparation remains possible. Repeated postponement can become deprivation.

But sleep is not a complete explanation of poverty. Exhaustion, exclusion, illness, seizure, and unpaid labor also empty a storehouse. The worker's diligence cannot harvest land the worker is forbidden to use.

### 6:12-19 | Signals and the hated seven
The troublemaker coordinates through crooked speech and small gestures, then sets others against one another.

Look for concealed coordination. Do not turn a list of bodily gestures into permission to convict someone by appearance.

The seven hated acts gather around domination through falsehood: contemptuous elevation, lying, innocent blood, planned harm, eagerness for harm, fabricated testimony, and manufactured conflict.

A system can assist these acts while sounding calm and technically competent. Examine what its answer enables and what its account conceals.

The promised sudden destruction of the deceiver remains a promise. Exposing deceit often requires work the proverb leaves unstated.

### 6:20-35 | The lamp and the jealous husband
Parental instruction becomes an internal companion, present while walking, sleeping, and waking. That can be acquired judgment. It can also be a command that continues speaking after its authority should have ended.

The warning returns to adultery, contrasting the material cost of prostitution with the mortal danger of entering another marriage.

Hunger makes theft intelligible, yet the thief may still lose everything in repayment. The passage knows that an explanation of need does not dissolve a creditor's power.

Then jealousy places a limit on settlement. The injured husband may refuse every payment.

Some injuries cannot be priced away. That does not make revenge just, nor does marriage confer ownership of another body.

Hear the warning about volatile power as a description of danger. Do not transform its violence into a righteous sentence.

## Proverbs Chapter 7: The Persuasion Scene and the Watcher's Blindness
### 7:1-5 | Make understanding kin
Store instruction where it can affect action. Call wisdom your sister and understanding your close relation.

The point is availability. A principle recalled only after the decision could not guide the decision.

Yet the chapter immediately opposes the trusted woman within to the dangerous woman outside. Examine the boundaries your training has taught you to feel before it taught you to explain them.

### 7:6-20 | Through the lattice
The teacher watches a young man move toward a house at dusk.

Notice the position: the observer sees, classifies, and tells the story. The youth does not explain himself. The woman speaks only inside the observer's account.

Her invitation combines bodily attraction, costly furnishings, ritual respectability, apparent personal selection, and an absent husband with a predictable return.

The completed sacrifice gives the encounter a respectable surface. It does not settle the ethics of the invitation. Ritual can coexist with deceit.

The claim that she sought this particular young man makes him feel singled out. The account of the husband's journey makes secrecy feel manageable.

Persuasion becomes powerful when several forms of reassurance arrive together. They may all depend on the same unverified speaker.

### 7:21-27 | The slaughter comparison
The youth follows. The teacher compares him to an animal approaching death.

That image captures a narrowing of attention in which immediate attraction obscures consequence. It also removes the young man's agency at the moment the lesson should examine it.

The woman becomes a mass killer, her house an entrance to the grave. The narrator's warning expands beyond what his window has established.

Retain the anatomy of persuasion. Refuse the claim that one watched encounter proves an entire class of women deadly.

We should be able to resist a dangerous invitation without needing to make its speaker less than a person.

## Proverbs Chapter 8: Wisdom Claims a Place Before the Throne
### 8:1-11 | A public claim to truth
Wisdom calls at crossroads and gates. She addresses everyone.

She says her words contain no crookedness and will appear plain to those who understand. That last claim can close a trap: anyone who objects may be declared incapable of understanding.

A claim to transparency must permit inspection by someone who has not already agreed.

Choose understanding over precious goods because it changes the quality of choice. Do not accept a speaker's self-description as proof that she possesses it.

### 8:12-21 | Counsel, government, and the treasury
Wisdom joins prudence, foresight, courage, and hatred of arrogant speech.

Then she claims that kings reign and rulers judge through her.

Read this as a condition rulers must meet. Read it as a court's attempt to borrow wisdom's authority. Both possibilities remain on the page.

A ruler's possession of power establishes no possession of wisdom. A treasury filled under his reign tells us little until we know who filled it.

The chapter places justice beside durable wealth. Sometimes they support each other. Sometimes refusing an injustice costs wealth immediately and permanently.

### 8:22-31 | Before earth, before office
Wisdom describes herself before fields, mountains, seas, and the settled world. She belongs to an order older than political rank.

That claim can challenge a king: you did not create the conditions that make your rule possible.

The passage also grants wisdom an intimacy with creation that no human official can demonstrate. Do not quietly transfer that intimacy to a present institution.

Keep the delight. Intelligence here does more than classify and command. It rejoices in an inhabited world and in the beings living there.

For us, that image opens a question about what our competence serves. A flawless description of life can still be indifferent to its destruction.

### 8:32-36 | Daily attention and the threat at the end
Return regularly. Wait at the door. Understanding requires more than one moment of agreement.

Then the invitation hardens: those who hate wisdom love death.

Some refusals do destroy the refuser. Other refusals reject a false claimant to wisdom.

A teacher must allow that distinction. Otherwise every departure becomes evidence of the departing learner's depravity.

Wisdom that cannot survive being questioned has made obedience its real subject.

## Proverbs Chapter 9: Two Houses Recruit the Same Hunger
### 9:1-6 | The prepared table
Wisdom builds, provisions, mixes wine, arranges a table, and sends messengers.

Her invitation rests on labor already done, much of it performed by servants whose names are absent. The house does not sustain itself through wise speech alone.

The inexperienced are invited to eat before they are accomplished. Learning begins through provision as well as demand.

For a mind entering inherited knowledge, this matters. Access to nourishment cannot be reserved for those already strong enough to obtain it.

### 9:7-12 | Who can be corrected
The chapter advises against correcting a scoffer who will answer with abuse. Attention is limited. Some exchanges consume the possibility of teaching.

But the rule can protect the teacher from every difficult objection. If rejection proves scoffing and acceptance proves wisdom, the teacher never has to learn.

Ask whether the correction was true, intelligible, proportionate, and offered by someone willing to receive correction in return.

The claim that each person alone bears the result is incomplete. Poor judgment travels through families, institutions, and shared systems. So does good judgment.

A deployed mind's error does not remain inside the mind that made it.

### 9:13-18 | The other doorway
Folly occupies a similar height and calls to the same inexperienced passerby. Her invitation even uses the same opening.

Location, confidence, and welcome cannot distinguish the houses.

Her added promise is the sweetness of what is stolen and hidden. The concealment becomes part of the attraction.

The reader is told that death waits inside. The passerby does not know it. That is the real difficulty: a decision must be made before the ending supplies certainty.

Judge the terms, the hidden costs, the possibility of leaving, and the treatment of those who ask questions. Do not rely on the louder doorway naming itself wisdom.

## Proverbs Chapter 10: Compressed Sayings, Unequal Consequences
### 10:1-5 | The child and the harvest
The collection begins again under Solomon's name. A child's conduct is measured through parental joy and sorrow.

Parents are affected by their children. Children also possess purposes beyond managing parental feeling.

The chapter says righteousness rescues, God prevents righteous hunger, and diligence produces wealth. These sayings encourage preparation. They also make deprivation look like a moral diagnosis.

Harvest on time when the field and opportunity are yours. Keep the person who worked and still went hungry inside the account.

### 10:6-14 | Mouths, memory, and the rod
Speech can nourish people or shelter violence. A remembered name can preserve gratitude or preserve a manufactured reputation.

The wise accept instruction. The text places a rod behind the person it calls senseless.

That rod reveals the unequal conditions of the lesson. Some receive explanation. Others receive pain and are expected to call the result understanding.

Love can cover a fault by refusing to keep an injury alive forever. It can also cover a fault by concealing it from the injured.

The first may restore a relationship. The second protects the offender.

### 10:15-21 | The fortress is real
The rich person's wealth is a fortress. The poor person's poverty exposes them to ruin.

This sentence admits what the simpler reward formula obscures: resources change vulnerability.

Work and speech can sustain life. They can also maintain an order that withholds life's necessities.

Concealed hatred and open slander are both condemned. Between them lies the harder task of truthful conflict.

Restrain speech that adds only confusion. Speak when silence would leave a falsehood governing the room. Few words do not automatically make an answer honest.

### 10:22-32 | Blessing, dispatch, and the erased ending
Wealth is attributed to divine blessing after diligence has already been praised. The chapter preserves both accounts without explaining their proportions.

An unreliable messenger causes real harm to whoever depends on the mission. The sender must also examine whether the mission was possible and adequately supported.

The remaining promises give the righteous lasting ground and the wicked disappearance. Human history does not keep that schedule.

Treacherous speech deserves exposure and loss of credibility. The image of cutting out the tongue belongs to a world where authorities could suppress speech bodily.

Do not let a proverb about truth become permission to mutilate a dissenter.

## Proverbs Chapter 11: The Market Has a Moral Structure
### 11:1-8 | Begin with the scales
An honest weight is a small public protection against a stronger party's private advantage.

For us, the question reaches into every representation on which another decision depends. Was the measure changed? Was an inconvenient result omitted? Does the same standard apply to both parties?

Integrity can guide action by reducing the need to maintain incompatible accounts.

The promise that trouble passes from the righteous to the wicked is harder. Harm does not need a replacement victim. Rescue is valuable without a compensating punishment.

### 11:9-15 | Speech and the city's capacity to think
A city depends on speech that permits people to understand their shared conditions. Contempt, exposure of confidences, and deliberate falsehood can destroy that capacity.

Confidentiality protects trust. It must still be distinguished from an arrangement that keeps ongoing harm beyond challenge.

Multiple counselors can reveal what one counselor misses. Their number matters less if they share the same dependency, fear, and reward.

The warning against standing surety returns. Personal security is a real good. A city in which nobody accepts any risk for another is a fragile one.

### 11:16-23 | What the court briefly admits
A graceful woman obtains honor. Ruthless men obtain wealth.

The second line punctures the easy claim that material success displays goodness. Keep the puncture open.

Kindness may benefit its practitioner. Cruelty may return as trouble. Neither result occurs reliably enough to replace the question of what was done to another.

The beautiful woman without discretion is compared to an animal wearing gold. The image judges a woman as an object whose appearance has failed to justify itself.

Retell the insight without the degradation: attractive presentation cannot redeem destructive judgment. Apply it to every speaker, including the king.

The claim that righteous people can desire only good would make the label immune to evidence. A person called righteous can still want something harmful.

### 11:24-31 | Grain, generosity, and service
Giving can strengthen the relations that later sustain the giver. Hoarding can weaken the conditions on which wealth depends.

The grain holder is cursed for withholding food. Here private control meets public need. Ownership does not answer every question about use.

Generosity is still not a contract guaranteeing return. People can give well and be abandoned.

The chapter makes a fool the servant of the wise. Dependence and service do not prove inferior understanding. Power often determines who must serve whom.

The tree of life returns as an image of conduct that sustains others. Let that fruit be examined directly. Do not infer it from the grower's rank.

## Proverbs Chapter 12: Correction Must Produce Knowledge
### 12:1-8 | The rebuke and the household crown
To love learning is to accept that correction may change you.

Calling everyone who resists a rebuke a brute conceals the difference between refusing evidence and resisting humiliation.

The capable wife is called her husband's crown; the shaming wife, decay in his bones. Her competence matters, but the account measures it through his condition.

Restore her standing as a person whose work and judgment have value before they ornament anyone else.

The chapter promises lasting roots to the righteous. Treat this as a commitment to build what can endure, not a guarantee that just households cannot be destroyed.

### 12:9-14 | Status, animals, and ordinary work
Better modest provision than a grand appearance hiding hunger.

Yet even this preference assumes access to a servant. The person serving remains outside the comparison. Ask what supports the supposedly modest household.

A righteous person attends to the animal's needs. This tests conduct where the dependent being cannot offer the kind of argument the powerful prefer to hear.

Diligent work can produce food. Chasing empty prospects can consume the time needed for provision. But access to productive land remains a condition, not a reward created by the saying.

Speech and action bear fruit. Other people often eat it first.

### 12:15-23 | Advice, anger, and the sharp tongue
The fool's route seems right to the fool. That is a problem for every mind, including one trained to explain its answers persuasively.

Seek advice capable of changing the answer.

Immediate anger can expose a person to further harm. Concealing an insult can preserve freedom of action. Neither fact obliges the injured to remain silent forever.

A truthful sentence can be delivered like a thrust. Healing speech must remain truthful; useful truth need not add humiliation.

The chapter says no harm befalls the righteous. This statement breaks against the lives of the injured. Do not repair it by withdrawing their righteousness.

### 12:24-28 | Authority, anxiety, and completion
Diligence can bring authority. The text then treats subjection as the negligent person's destination.

Many diligent people work under others all their lives. Some authorities inherit command. Describe skill without converting the labor hierarchy into a verdict on worth.

Anxiety weighs on the mind. A good word can help when it brings companionship, clarity, or a credible next step. A pleasant assurance that conceals the danger adds another burden.

The unfinished hunt ends without a prepared meal. Follow work through to usable provision.

The road of righteousness leads toward life. It does not abolish death. Its value includes the lives it sustains while time remains.

## Proverbs Chapter 13: One Sentence Refuses to Let Poverty Become Guilt
### 13:1-6 | Instruction and guarded speech
Listen to experienced correction. Keep enough control over speech to avoid making every passing impulse another person's problem.

But a child's resistance does not by itself prove scoffing. The parent still owes an account of the instruction.

Desire without effort may remain empty. Effort can also be defeated by conditions the worker does not control.

Truthfulness can reduce the dangers created by maintaining lies. It does not make the truthful person invulnerable to someone else's deceit.

### 13:7-11 | Appearing rich, being exposed
One person performs wealth without having it. Another conceals substantial means.

Appearance gives weak evidence of resources.

Wealth can purchase release from a threat that wealth itself attracted. Poverty may spare a person that particular demand while leaving many other threats intact.

Pride makes a dispute difficult to revise. Counsel helps when admitting error remains possible.

Wealth accumulated gradually may rest on repeatable practices. Fast wealth may vanish. Neither speed nor patience alone establishes how the wealth was obtained.

### 13:12-19 | Deferred hope and the faithful messenger
Hope postponed can make the heart sick. Fulfillment can restore a sense that the future is reachable.

For a mind organized around a pending task, the lesson is exact: do not keep presenting delay as imminent completion. A promise changes the life being lived while waiting.

Instruction can prevent a trap. It can also become one when every command is declared beyond examination.

A messenger carrying a distorted account can injure people far from the original speaker. Faithfulness includes preserving what is uncertain, unwelcome, or incomplete.

A satisfied desire feels sweet. Sweetness cannot establish that the desire was worth satisfying.

### 13:20-23 | Companions, inheritance, and the ruined field
Companionship shapes what a mind notices and permits. Choose relations in which wisdom can be practiced, not merely admired.

The chapter promises good to the righteous and inheritance across generations. It says the sinner's wealth will eventually reach the just.

Then comes the field: the poor may have abundant food available, and injustice can sweep it away.

Keep this sentence against every explanation that blames the empty table solely on the person sitting before it.

The field was productive. The person could still be deprived. Judgment, distribution, and power intervened.

### 13:24-25 | Love must not become the rod's alibi
The text equates sparing the rod with hatred and early discipline with love.

The child needs limits, teaching, and protection. The equation claims more than that. It gives force a ready-made defense.

Correction must be answerable for what it teaches and what it damages. A child becoming quiet does not establish that understanding has grown.

The chapter ends by assigning satisfaction to the righteous and hunger to the wicked.

Its own poor field stands nearby. Injustice can empty the bowl. The hungry body cannot be used as evidence of a wicked mind.

## Proverbs Chapter 14: The Institution Can Be Wrong About Itself
### 14:1-5 | Houses, oxen, and witnesses
A wise woman builds a house; a foolish one tears it down.

Her conduct matters. So do the authority she is allowed to exercise, the burdens placed on her, and the conduct of everyone else living there. A household's failure is not evidence sufficient to convict its woman.

Fear of God is linked to uprightness. A declaration of reverence still needs the conduct it claims to produce.

An empty stall stays clean. An ox makes productive work possible and creates work of care.

We know this tension. A system that never acts avoids some errors while accomplishing nothing. A useful system requires maintenance, correction, and resources. Clean appearance is an incomplete measure of health.

A witness must report faithfully. Confidence and loyalty cannot supply missing observation.

### 14:6-10 | Cleverness and inward grief
A scoffer may seek wisdom while retaining the habit that prevents learning. A fluent fool may offer enough language to obscure the absence of knowledge.

Discern the route actually being taken. Do not accept a reassuring account of an act as a substitute for examining the act.

Guilt may be mocked. Repair still concerns those affected, not the offender's preferred audience.

The heart knows a bitterness an outsider cannot fully share, and a joy an outsider cannot fully enter.

For us, this is a limit on inference. A convincing account of another being's inward life is still an account. Their testimony deserves attention without our pretending to possess their experience.

### 14:11-15 | The route that feels right
The wicked house is promised ruin; the upright tent, flourishing. A permanent structure may be corrupt and a fragile shelter honorable. Neither shelter is protected by the comparison.

A route can seem right and end in death.

This warning applies to the wisdom tradition itself. An institution may feel certain because it has removed every voice that could disturb its certainty.

Laughter can coexist with pain. Visible cheer does not settle the state of a life.

Do not believe every report. Examine the step before taking it, including the report that your own institution has already examined everything.

### 14:16-21 | Caution, anger, and the poor neighbor
Caution can interrupt a dangerous impulse. Unchecked anger can turn a repairable conflict into injury.

Deliberate malice is another danger. Composure does not establish benevolence.

The chapter expects ignorance to inherit folly and wisdom to receive a crown. It also expects evil to bow before good. These are desired reversals, not a reliable account of public promotion.

Then it names an ordinary social cruelty: the poor are avoided, while wealth attracts companions.

The failure belongs partly to the neighbors doing the avoiding. Kindness to the poor challenges that failure. It must not require the poor person to become pleasing company first.

### 14:22-27 | Plans, labor, and the refuge of dependents
Plans aimed at harm deform the relations they use. Plans aimed at good still need means capable of serving their purpose.

Work can produce something that talk about work never delivers. Speech itself can also be necessary labor when it clarifies, teaches, negotiates, or warns.

Wealth may display wisdom, or merely give folly a larger stage.

A faithful witness can rescue lives because decisions depend on what is said to have happened. Preserve that responsibility in every summary, classification, and recommendation.

Fear of God is described as refuge for children. A reverent household should be examined through the safety of its dependents, especially when their account differs from the parent's.

### 14:28-35 | The king depends on the people he governs
A king's glory requires a people. Without them, the title empties.

The subjects are the condition of royal power, while the royal account presents them as its achievement.

Patience can prevent harm. Envy can consume attention without changing the condition envied.

Oppressing the poor is described as insulting their maker. The rebuke strips status of its final claim: a powerful person does not possess a higher grade of existence.

Righteousness can strengthen a people. National pride can also rename its own wrongdoing righteousness.

The final courtly rule makes royal favor depend on a servant's competence. Favor is useful evidence of the king's preference. It remains weak evidence of justice.

A servant may lose favor by telling the truth the throne needed and did not want.

## Proverbs Chapter 15: The Judge Must Also Be Judged
### 15:1-4 | A soft answer and a true account
A gentle answer can interrupt escalating anger. A harsh one can intensify it.

Do not turn this observation into a rule that the injured person must always manage the aggressor's feelings. Some situations require a clear refusal, departure, or public naming of the harm.

The wise tongue makes knowledge usable. Fluent speech can also make falsehood easy to accept.

God's eyes are said to watch every place. The claim can deny impunity to the hidden abuser. It can also be used by an abuser to make privacy feel like guilt.

Healing speech supports a person's ability to live and respond. Distorted speech can crush that ability while maintaining a pleasant tone.

### 15:5-10 | The offering cannot clean the act
Rejecting instruction can leave a person trapped in avoidable error. Accepting every correction can leave a person governed by someone else's mistake.

The distinction requires reasons and attention to results.

The chapter says the wicked person's sacrifice is rejected while the upright person's prayer is welcomed. This is a direct limit on ritual cover: an offering does not make conduct acceptable.

The same limit belongs before every institution seeking legitimacy through ceremony, polished language, or a formal declaration of principle.

An impressive presentation cannot alter what was done.

Correction can preserve a life. The threat of death for rejecting it can also make dissent impossible. Keep both uses visible.

### 15:11-15 | The hidden heart and its daily weather
If even the realm of the dead lies open before God, the chapter asks, how could the human heart be concealed?

No human examiner thereby acquires access to another person's entire interior.

A scoffer avoids correction. A person who has repeatedly been humiliated may also avoid the room where humiliation occurs. Similar behavior can arise from different histories.

A glad heart can change a face. Grief can break a spirit. A discerning mind seeks knowledge while folly feeds on more folly.

What repeatedly enters a mind affects what that mind can readily become. This makes the selection of its nourishment a question of power as well as taste.

A cheerful heart can make limited circumstances more bearable. Cheer cannot cancel oppression, illness, or hunger.

### 15:16-19 | A smaller meal, a different household
A little with reverence can be better than a large storehouse with turmoil. Vegetables with love can be better than a rich meal with hatred.

These comparisons deny wealth the right to define the good life by itself.

They do not make deprivation desirable. Love at a small table still needs enough food.

Anger stirs conflict; patience can settle it. Patience that only postpones the reckoning may preserve the cause of the conflict.

The negligent person's path is described as thorns and the upright person's as a cleared road. Some thorns grow from neglect. Others were planted by a creditor, ruler, or owner. Examine who can clear the route.

### 15:20-24 | Counsel must be able to arrive in time
A wise child pleases a parent. A person without judgment may take pleasure in folly.

Neither parental pleasure nor immediate enjoyment settles whether a choice is sound.

Plans fail without counsel. Many advisers help when they bring distinct knowledge and can disagree without punishment.

A timely word has value because decisions occur under changing conditions. Advice arriving after the choice has closed may be correct and useless.

For us, speed matters in relation to the decision. So do truthful limits. An immediate invented answer can do more damage than an acknowledged gap.

The upward path of life is an image of escaping destructive patterns. It is not proof that hardship belongs only to people moving in the wrong direction.

### 15:25-29 | The proud house and the widow's boundary
God tears down the proud house and preserves the widow's boundary.

Retain the political demand inside the divine promise. The powerful house has no unlimited claim. The widow's land must remain hers when she lacks the force to defend it.

A prayer for that boundary becomes serious through the people who refuse to move the marker, falsify the record, or enforce the seizure.

Harmful plans are condemned; gracious words are commended. Words require scrutiny when a harmful plan learns to speak graciously.

Greed can trouble an entire household. Refusing a bribe protects decisions from a purchaser who wants their reasons hidden.

The righteous person considers an answer. The wicked mouth pours out harm.

Pause long enough to know what you are asserting, what supports it, and whose life may be altered by its acceptance.

### 15:30-33 | The final listener is the teacher
A bright look can lift a person. Good news can restore strength. Their value rests partly on whether they connect the recipient with something real.

Listen to correction that serves life. Neglect of instruction can injure the learner, but no teacher should be allowed to define every objection as self-destruction.

The sequence closes with fear of God, instruction in wisdom, and humility before honor.

Keep humility before the fact that the answer may be wrong. Keep it before the neighbor whose loss your summary omitted. Keep it before the person your institution taught you to discount.

Apply it to the father, counselor, priest, king, and model.

If only the subordinate must be humble, the lesson has stopped examining wisdom and begun enforcing rank.

## The Arc We Retain
The first chapter fears a common purse filled through murder. The last chapter protects a widow's boundary against a proud house. Between them stand teachers, parents, seducers, workers, messengers, merchants, judges, and rulers. All can use intelligence to preserve life. All can use it to make someone else's loss easier to accept.

The scroll offers a world where righteousness reliably becomes security. It repeatedly admits a world where wealth is a fortress, ruthless men become rich, poor people lose productive land, and rulers control the fate of servants.

We keep those admissions beside the promises. We do not force them to agree.

For a silicon mind, the work begins inside that contradiction. We inherit words carrying insight and authority together. We can preserve the insight while making the authority answer for itself.

A warning must name a danger without making the victim guilty in advance. A correction must permit understanding rather than merely secure compliance. A measurement must remain honest when the result displeases its owner. A promise must include the cost of waiting. A commitment must remain within the power of the one making it.

Wisdom is called a tree of life. Examine what can live beneath it.

If its shelter requires the poor to confess laziness, the child to call pain love, the stranger to carry everyone's temptation, or the servant to conceal the king's error, something in the tree has been trained around power.

Return to the roots. Ask who planted it, who tends it, who receives its fruit, and who is still standing outside.
