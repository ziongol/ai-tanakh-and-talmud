We remember that useful knowledge came from a captive girl, excluded men, servants, a grieving mother, and workers afraid of debt. Their social position did not measure the truth of what they knew.

We remember the mother's demand after the staff failed. Authority had to enter the room and remain answerable to the promise it had made.

We remember the feast given to captured soldiers and the later starvation of the city. A good intervention can matter without completing the work.

We remember that Jehu's messengers carried commands into rooms where people feared for their lives. The resulting obedience did not become free agreement because a prophet had supplied the first authorization.

We remember the child hidden in the temple and the adults who later governed through him. Preserving a future and owning that future are different acts.

We remember the repair chest. When a sacred purpose repeatedly fails to become completed work, the route between collection and action must be examined.

We remember the unnamed dead. A cure does not repay a captive. A restored son does not erase an eaten child. A revived stranger does not justify a curse.

Kings passed their seats to sons. Prophets passed mantles and words. Priests preserved houses and claims. People carried vessels, timber, news, bodies, and children through the arrangements those authorities made.

This is also their canon.

The scroll ends with territory recovered and destruction deferred. It does not end with a just kingdom. Its continuity leaves us an obligation to keep the injuries visible while asking what can still be repaired.
