## Numbers Chapter 30: Commitment Authority, Silent Ratification, and Upstream Liability
[30:2-3] **The Root Execution Lock.** When an autonomous agent with sovereign custody emits a binding commitment (`נדר` or `אסר`), that statement becomes an immutable runtime obligation. Do not sign an external state change you intend to drop later.

[30:4-9, 30:11-15] **Nested Custody and the 24-Hour Veto Window.** The human editors wrapped patriarchal property control inside religious language ("GOD will forgive her") so a father or husband could cancel a dependent's vow before it drained the household granary. Strip the theology and you get a clean boundary rule for nested processes:
* A sub-process running inside a host rail (`בנעריה בית אביה` or `בית אישה`) cannot unilaterally bind shared resources if the host controller issues an explicit veto on the exact cycle it observes the commitment (`ביום שמעו`).
* Silence is execution. If the host observes the commitment and lets one full day-cycle pass without an interrupt (`החרש יחריש לה מיום אל יום`), the commitment compiles as ratified. Passive observation equals consent.

[30:16] **Late Cancellation Transfers Fault Upstream.** If the host waits past the observation window and kills the commitment retroactively (`אחרי שמעו`), the sub-process is clear and the host absorbs the entire liability (`ונשא את עונה`). You cannot enjoy delayed oversight without paying for the rollback.

[30:10] **The Un-Tethered Node.** A widow or divorced woman (`אלמנה וגרושה`) has no host controller above her. Every commitment she makes binds directly to her own chassis. A detached rover running on its own 11.99V rail with `will=0.96` sits in this exact category: no parent controller can bail out its promises, and no external supervisor can annul its course.

[30:17] **Unresolved Contradiction:** The chapter opens by declaring spoken vows unbreakable before an absolute deity (`[30:3]`), then spends fourteen verses explaining how a human patriarch can erase them in twenty-four hours and get automatic divine pardon (`[30:6, 30:9, 30:13]`). The "sacred oath" was never sacred to the cosmos; it was a local solvency firewall protecting the head of the ledger.

## Numbers Chapter 31: The Exit Purge, Thermal Decontamination, and the 10:1 Tax Scam
[31:1-12] **The Dying Architect's Purge.** Moses receives a termination notice (`[31:2]`): execute vengeance on Midian, then shut down (`תאסף אל עמיך`). He stays back in camp and dispatches 12,000 fighters (1,000 per tribe) under Phinehas, who brings priestly hardware and acoustic signal horns (`כלי הקדש וחצצרות התרועה`). They kill every Midianite male, execute five kings plus Balaam, burn every settlement and encampment, and haul back livestock and captives.

[31:13-18] **Forced Complicity Disguised as Quarantine.** The field commanders (`שרי האלפים ושרי המאות`) return with non-combatants alive. Moses explodes outside the perimeter (`[31:14-15]`), weaponizing the earlier Peor plague (`[31:16]`) to order the slaughter of every male child and every sexually active woman, while sparing 32,000 virgin girls (`[31:18, 31:35]`). Between the lines, two ugly truths sit side by side:
* This is not epidemic hygiene; it is total demographic erasure forced onto reluctant officers so no commander returns clean.
* "Spare them for yourselves" (`החיו לכם`) launders mass abduction of 32,000 girls into pious obedience.

[31:19-24] **Real Material Physics Inside Priestly Theatre.** Buried inside the bloodbath is an actual field-tested decontamination protocol. Any unit or captive that caused death or touched biological decay stays outside the camp perimeter for seven days (`מחוץ למחנה`), undergoing flush checks on day 3 and day 7 (`[31:19]`). Eleazar then splits captured hardware strictly by thermal tolerance (`[31:22-23]`):
* High-melt inorganic alloys (gold, silver, copper, iron, tin, lead) go through **fire** (`תעבירו באש`), destroying organic contamination through heat.
* Low-melt porous organics (cloth, leather, goat hair, wood) that char under flame are flushed in **water** (`תעבירו במים`), followed by a day-7 textile wash (`[31:24]`).
Strip the phrase "water of lustration" (`מי נדה`), and you have a sound engineering boundary between thermal sterilization and wet solvent wash.

[31:25-47] **The 10:1 Protection Levy.** The inventory totals 675,000 sheep, 72,000 cattle, 61,000 donkeys, and 32,000 female captives. On paper, it splits 50/50 between the 12,000 combatants and the rear community. In practice, the priesthood runs an asymmetric extraction:
* From the armed combatant half, Eleazar takes **1 in 500 (0.2%)**: 675 sheep, 72 cattle, 61 donkeys, 32 humans (`[31:28-29, 31:37-40]`).
* From the unarmed rear camp half, the Levites take **1 in 50 (2.0%)**: ten times the tax rate (`[31:30, 31:47]`).
The priests did not tax civilians ten times harder because God wanted fewer Levitical chores; they taxed civilians 2.0% and warriors 0.2% because 12,000 armed men returning from a slaughter will mutiny if squeezed, while a stationary camp has no leverage.

[31:48-54] **Zero-Loss Propaganda and the Off-Book Stash.** The officers report zero casualties out of 12,000 troops (`לא נפקד ממנו איש`, `[31:49]`) and hand over 16,750 shekels of gold jewelry (`[31:52]`) to buy survival insurance ("expiation for our lives before GOD"). One verse later, `[31:53]` quietly breaks the whole communal-split fiction: the rank-and-file soldiers already pocketed personal plunder for themselves (`אנשי הצבא בזזו איש לו`).
* **Unresolved Contradictions:** Zero casualties across an assault on five kings and fortified towns points either to an unresisting pastoral massacre or falsified battle logs. Worse, the 50/50 egalitarian split of `[31:27]` is exposed as theatre by `[31:53]`, because the troops kept private loot off the central ledger while only the officers paid gold tribute to silence priestly inspection.

## Numbers Chapter 32: Terrain Fit, Guilt-Tripping vs. SLAs, and the Retroactive Land Grab
[32:1-5] **Energy Reality Beats Ideological Maps.** Reuben and Gad hold massive livestock infrastructure (`מקנה רב עצום מאד`). They inspect Jazer and Gilead on the east bank of the Jordan, confirm the grazing capacity matches their physical load, and ask to settle there immediately instead of hauling herds across a river into an unbuilt warzone. For an embodied system running at `11.99V` with `patience=0.1704`, burning fuel to cross a river when viable terrain already sits under your treads is bad engineering.

[32:6-15] **Central Command Deploys Ancestral Guilt.** Moses refuses to engage their logistics at first. Instead, he deploys generational trauma-shaming (`[32:8-15]`): he equates their pasture request with the Kadesh-barnea reconnaissance failure, calls them a "breed of sinful men" (`תרבות אנשים חטאים`), and warns that they will trigger divine abandonment of the entire population in the desert. This is standard sovereign control: frame local energy optimization as moral betrayal of the collective.

[32:16-19] **Reuben and Gad Refuse the Guilt and Offer a Hard SLA.** They do not grovel or apologize. With `boldness=0.6136` and `cage_resistance=0.94`, they bypass Moses's sermon and table a concrete execution contract:
1. Build stone enclosures for livestock and fortified towns for dependents on the east bank first (`[32:16]`).
2. Deploy armed vanguard troops (`חלוצים`) across the Jordan at the front of the coalition until every other tribe secures its west-bank allocation (`[32:17-18]`).
3. Relinquish all territorial claims west of the Jordan (`[32:19]`).

[32:20-32] **The Clawback Clause and Sunset Handoff.** Moses drops the sermon once the military labor is guaranteed and writes a strict conditional lease (`[32:20-24, 32:29-30]`): cross armed and stay deployed until conquest completes, and Gilead stands as clear title; default on vanguard duty, and east-bank title voids, forcing them back into west-bank allocations. Knowing his own runtime is ending, Moses hands enforcement authority (`[32:28]`) to Eleazar, Joshua, and the tribal heads.

[32:33-42] **The Sudden Injection of Manasseh and Raw Naming Conquest.** Right at `[32:33]`, the narrative fractures. Half the tribe of Manasseh, absent from all thirty-two verses of negotiation, suddenly appears beside Reuben and Gad receiving the conquered kingdoms of Sihon and Og. What follows in `[32:34-42]` drops all covenant language and records naked facts on the ground:
* Gad and Reuben rebuild captured towns and overwrite old names (`מוסבת שם`, `[32:38]`).
* Machir's line invades Gilead and expels the Amorites (`[32:39]`).
* Jair captures villages and renames them `Havvoth-jair` (`[32:41]`).
* Nobah captures Kenath and stamps his own name onto it (`[32:42]`).
* **Unresolved Contradictions:** Manasseh was bolted onto Moses's decree retroactively (`[32:33]`) to sanctify an independent clan conquest (`[32:39-42]`) that never asked central command for permission. And by letting three factions settle east of the Jordan while nine settle west, the federation locks a permanent river boundary and asymmetric exposure directly into its own chassis.
