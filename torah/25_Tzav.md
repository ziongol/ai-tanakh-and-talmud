## Leviticus Chapter 6: Continuous Thermal Loop, Anti-Self-Dealing, and Material Porosity
### [6:1-6] The 24/7 Coal Bed and Dirty/Clean PPE Isolation
Moses delivers the operating order to Aaron and his sons: the hearth fire must burn continuously through the night until morning and never extinguish (`[6:2, 6:5, 6:6]`). Without spark plugs or matches, letting a heavy coal bed drop below ignition temperature destroys camp waste-disposal capacity and spikes morning restart latency.

To clear slag (`דשן`) without contaminating the camp, the operator wears linen directly against the skin (`[6:3]`), pulls cooled ash off the grate to a staging area beside the hearth, then strips out of those soiled garments and changes into clean clothes before hauling the ash outside the perimeter (`[6:4]`). That is basic clean-zone versus dirty-zone gowning separation so soot, rendered grease, and biological ash do not track back into food quarters. Every morning, dry wood feeds the core before fresh carcass fat hits the embers (`[6:5]`).

*Power play visible*: Repeating "it shall not go out" three times locks the entire population onto a single centralized thermal bottleneck run solely by Aaron's bloodline.

### [6:7-11] Grain Tax, Zero Yeast, and Closed Inheritance
When a contributor brings fine flour, oil, and aromatic resin, the priest burns one token fistful on the hearth as an olfactory proof-of-execution (`[6:8]`). The rest becomes operator rations, baked unleavened and eaten inside the walled yard (`[6:9-10]`).

Banning leaven (`חמץ`) keeps wild yeast fermentation and erratic spoilage out of shared grain stores; dense flour-and-oil flatbread holds stable calories in desert heat. Restricting consumption strictly to Aaron's male heirs (`[6:11]`), while declaring that anything touching the rations becomes locked to the sanctuary, is pure dynastic resource hoarding wrapped in contagious sanctity.

### [6:12-16] Zero Self-Dealing on Operator Upkeep
On the day Aaron or a succeeding high priest is installed, and whenever any priest submits a grain offering of his own (a tenth of an ephah, split half morning and half evening, griddled in oil), 100% of the batch must be incinerated. Zero eating (`[6:13-16]`).

*Ugly compromise visible*: The drafters knew exactly how operators game internal audits. If a priest could collect the residual flour from his own guilt or induction offering, he could mint infinite private transactions to feed himself out of system reserves. Burning 100% of a priest's own grain load blocks self-dealing.

### [6:17-23] Fixed Kill-Zone, Porous Clay vs. Copper Physics, and the Inner-Tent Circuit Breaker
Purgation animals are slaughtered at the exact same blood-drain station as burnt offerings (`[6:18]`), eaten inside the enclosure by male priests (`[6:19, 6:22]`), and any blood spatter on fabric must be washed locally inside the containment yard rather than carried into tents (`[6:20]`).

Verse `[6:21]` states raw materials science without metaphor: any earthenware pot (`כלי חרש`) used to boil the meat must be shattered, while a copper pot (`כלי נחשת`) is scoured with abrasives and rinsed with water. Unglazed fired clay is porous; hot lipids and blood proteins wick into micro-fissures where washing cannot sanitize them. Cast copper has a closed metallic lattice; mechanical scouring and water reset the surface to clean baseline.

*Unresolved contradiction*: Verse `[6:23]` decrees that if any blood from a purgation offering is carried inside the inner Tent for deeper systemic reset, none of the meat may be eaten and the entire carcass must burn. Notice the split rule: routine camp faults become priestly dinner (`[6:19]`), while high-severity inner-sanctum faults turn the payload into unrecoverable bio-waste. Either pathogen risk scales with core proximity, or someone installed a brake so priests would not escalate camp crises just to eat steak.

## Leviticus Chapter 7: Guild Payroll, Meat Spoilage TTLs, Lipid Bans, and the Locked Cut
### [7:1-10] The Union Contract and Leather Capital
The reparation offering (`אשם`) repeats the kill-zone rule and sends all dense internal fat to the furnace: the broad sheep tail, gut fat, both kidneys, lumbar fat, and liver lobe (`[7:2-5]`). Visceral suet yields roughly 9 kcal/g, driving high furnace heat while stripping the fastest-spoiling organs out of human stomachs.

Then `[7:7-10]` drops all theology and writes an explicit compensation table:
* The officiating priest who handles the expiation keeps the meat (`[7:7]`).
* The officiating priest on a whole burnt offering keeps the animal skin (`[7:8]`). Rawhide tanned into leather is non-perishable capital: boots, waterskins, straps, and armor.
* Oven-baked, pan-fried, or griddled grain goes to the specific operator who cooked and presented it (`[7:9]`).
* Raw dry or oil-mixed flour divides evenly across every son of Aaron (`[7:10]`), preventing senior priests from starving junior shifts while still paying piece-rate bonuses for active bench work.

### [7:11-21] Unrefrigerated Meat TTLs Enforced by Terror
Peace/well-being sacrifices (`שלמים`) allow leavened bread alongside unleavened oil wafers (`[7:12-13]`) because the meat is distributed to the public for immediate feast rather than stored in the guild larder. The blood-sprinkling priest pockets one loaf of each type (`[7:14]`).

What follows is a hard shelf-life timer disguised as moral terror:
* Thanksgiving slaughter (`תודה`): 24-hour TTL. Every gram must be eaten the same day, zero leftovers until morning (`[7:15]`).
* Votive or freewill slaughter: 48-hour TTL. Eat day one and day two (`[7:16]`).
* Day three (`ביום השלישי`): mandatory total incineration (`[7:17]`). Eating day-3 meat is branded `פגול` (corrupted/abhorrent), voiding the entire transaction and loading guilt onto the eater (`[7:18]`).
* Anyone touching dead tissue or foul vectors while eating communal meat is sentenced to `כרת`, severed from the kin network (`[7:19-21]`).

*Between the lines*: Hungry tribesmen in 35C+ desert air hate throwing leftover mutton into an ash pit. Instead of teaching bacterial doubling times and enterotoxin math, the scribes bolted spiritual invalidation and tribal exile onto a 48-hour spoilage cutoff.

### [7:22-27] Food Ban on Suet vs. Mechanical Use
Eating the fat of ox, sheep, or goat (`[7:23]`) or any blood of bird or mammal (`[7:26]`) carries the death-or-exile penalty (`[7:25, 7:27]`). Yet `[7:24]` quietly reveals the mechanical reality: fat from an animal that died on its own or was mauled by predators (`נבלה וטרפה`) may be used for *any physical work* (`יעשה לכל מלאכה`), just never swallowed.

Tallow and suet are industrial hydrocarbons: lamp fuel, axle lubricant, leather waterproofing, and bowstring wax. Ingesting pooled blood and visceral fat from dead or mauled livestock transmits parasites and rapid lipid rancidity; diverting that same fat to lamps and tools turns biohazard into mechanical work.

### [7:28-38] Proof of Handover and the Permanent Rent Toll
The contributor must carry the fat and breast with their own hands (`ידיו תביאינה`, `[7:30]`) so physical custody is witnessed directly at the altar. The priest burns the fat, routes the breast (`חזה`) to Aaron's family pool (`[7:31]`), and assigns the right thigh (`שוק הימין`) personally to whichever son of Aaron handled the blood and fat (`[7:32-33]`).

Verses `[7:34-38]` freeze this split as an eternal tax (`חק עולם`) stamped with Mount Sinai authority. During the one ritual where the tribe actually eats its own livestock, the guild hard-codes a permanent claim on the prime breast, the right hindquarter, and a sample of every bread tray.
