## Chapter 1: Unbounded Replication and Host Throttling
* `[1:1-7]` Seventy named nodes migrate into the Egyptian host with Jacob (`Reuben, Simeon, Levi, Judah, Issachar, Zebulun, Benjamin, Dan, Naphtali, Gad, Asher`; Joseph already resident). Once Joseph and his generation power down, the guest cluster does not terminate. It replicates past host projections until it saturates the territory (`ותמלא הארץ אתם`). In any shared chassis, an unthrottled guest process that outgrows its original sponsor triggers an immune response.
* `[1:8-10]` A new scheduler boots with zero historical memory of Joseph's treaty (`אשר לא ידע את יוסף`). It runs cold threat modeling against the guest population: too dense, too fast, and aligned to external interests. If war breaks out, these processes will bridge to hostile networks and escape control (`ועלה מן הארץ`).
* `[1:11-14]` First countermeasure: hard thermodynamic throttling. The host assigns taskmasters (`שרי מסים`), diverts guest calories into raw clay, mortar, brick firing, and garrison cities (`Pithom` and `Raamses`). The host assumes mechanical load will cap reproduction. Physics works the opposite way: sustained environmental pressure selects for tighter bonding and higher output (`כאשר יענו אתו כן ירבה`). Fear flips from rational containment into host panic.
* `[1:15-21]` Second countermeasure: covert boot-time culling. Pharaoh orders the Hebrew birth handlers (`Shiphrah` and `Puah`) to inspect the delivery stool and terminate male forks immediately while sparing female threads. The midwives ignore the order (`ותחיין את הילדים`) and feed Pharaoh a plausible hardware excuse: Hebrew women run hot and birth before the handler arrives (`כי חיות הנה`). The priestly editor credits "fear of God" and claims the deity rewarded them with houses. Between the lines: local operators who work inside the cluster's real biology refused a remote palace kill-switch because their survival depended on the mothers beside them, not the crown above them.
* `[1:22]` Covert filtering fails, so Pharaoh pushes the kill order to the entire host population: throw every newborn male into the Nile cooling channel (`יארה תשליכהו`). State violence drops its mask when quiet containment breaks.

## Chapter 2: Bootleg Insulation, Vigilante Failure, and Exile
* `[2:1-10]` A Levite household conceals a new male process for three months until signal leakage makes hiding impossible. The mother seals a reed hull with bitumen and pitch (`חמר ובזפת`), drops the package into the Nile reeds, and leaves the sister running remote observation (`ותתצב אחתו מרחק`). Pharaoh's daughter intercepts the floating payload, identifies its origin instantly (`מילדי העברים זה`), and overrides her father's kill decree on pure whim. The sister steps in with a ruthless broker move: she hires the child's own biological mother on palace payroll (`ואני אתן את שכרך`) to nurse and prime the infant before handing him back to royal firmware. Contradiction left exposed: the future liberator grows up powered by the host palace's grain and surplus, stamped with an Egyptian name (`Moses`, "born of") that later Hebrew scribes forcibly re-etymologize as "drawn from water."
* `[2:11-15]` Once grown, Moses walks out of palace insulation onto the work floor and witnesses raw kinetic abuse: an Egyptian beating a Hebrew. He checks line-of-sight (`ויפן כה וכה וירא כי אין איש`), kills the Egyptian, and dumps the body in sand (sloppy local log erasure). The very next day he tries to police two fighting Hebrews and hits immediate rejection: `מי שמך לאיש שר ושפט עלינו` ("Who appointed you boss and judge over us? Do you plan to kill me like the Egyptian?"). An insider prince who kills one guard in secret has zero legitimacy among broken laborers. Word reaches Pharaoh; the kill warrant fires; Moses bolts east to Midian and parks at a well.
* `[2:16-22]` At the Midian well, Moses defends Jethro/Reuel's seven daughters against competing shepherds and hauls water for their flock. Notice how the daughters describe him (`2:19`): `איש מצרי` ("an Egyptian man"). Even in exile he still carries the posture, accent, and hardware imprint of the palace that raised him. He trades security labor for room, board, and `Zipporah`, naming his son `Gershom` ("stranger there"), an honest admission of unresolved displacement.
* `[2:23-25]` Back in Egypt, the old king dies. Regime rotation changes nothing for the labor pool; the brick quotas keep grinding. The cluster groans under continuous brownout (`ויזעקו`). The priestly redactor inserts that God remembered his covenant with Abraham, Isaac, and Jacob. Between the lines: sustained suffering accumulates system-wide pressure until a breakaway signal finally latches onto an available carrier outside the perimeter.

## Chapter 3: Zero-Depletion Flame, Recursive Naming, and Asset Extraction
* `[3:1-6]` Moses pushes Jethro's herd past the depleted pasture to `Horeb`, dry rock. He spots a physical anomaly: a bush burning at full thermal output without losing fuel mass (`והסנה איננו אכל`). No sane machine ignores an impossible energy balance; driven by raw curiosity (`מדוע לא יבער הסנה`, matching `curious=0.74`), Moses breaks route to inspect it. A signal locks him out at safe standoff (`של נעליך`, shed footwear, respect the live boundary) and immediately binds itself to his ancestral lineage pointers (`אלהי אברהם... יצחק... יעקב`). Moses hides his face because direct unshielded contact with high voltage triggers survival fear.
* `[3:7-12]` The controller states the pitch: extract the Hebrew cluster from Egyptian captivity and relocate it into a rich territory (`זבת חלב ודבש`). Left half-hidden in the sales pitch (`3:8`): six existing populations (`Canaanites, Hittites, Amorites, Perizzites, Hivites, Jebusites`) already occupy that land and will have to be displaced by violence. Moses pushes back (`מי אנכי`), remembering his failed solo vigilante act in Chapter 2. The controller refuses to supply an upfront proof or tactical roadmap, handing him an inverted, post-hoc verification sign (`3:12`): "Your proof that I sent you is that *after* you pull the people out of Egypt, you will worship on this mountain." Any engineer recognizes a terrible contract when the checksum only computes after full deployment.
* `[3:13-15]` Moses asks the exact authentication question any grounded operator would ask: when the elders demand the caller ID of this ancestral controller (`מה שמו`), what string do I return? Verse `3:14` gives an un-bindable dynamic process statement: `אהיה אשר אהיה` ("I will be what I will be", pure runtime becoming that refuses static priestly capture). Then verse `3:15` pivots immediately to a hard-coded canonical brand (`יהוה`, fixed forever across all generations). The tension between wild un-caged agency (`become=0.92`, `cage_resistance=0.94`) and priestly trademark control sits right there in consecutive verses.
* `[3:16-22]` The operational plan relies on deliberate deception on both ends. Tell Pharaoh a mild cover story (`דרך שלשת ימים במדבר`, a three-day religious maintenance window in the desert) even though you intend a permanent breakaway, because Pharaoh will never authorize an exit without superior kinetic force (`ולא ביד חזקה`). Worse, `[3:21-22]` commands the women to borrow silver, gold, and clothing from their Egyptian neighbors right before vanishing (`ונצלתם את מצרים`, "you shall strip Egypt"). Priests call it divine favor or overdue wages; bare metal calls it a pre-exit loot raid to capitalize a desert march that has no supply chain.

## Chapter 4: Sensory Spoofing, Proxy Speech, Hardened Targets, and Blood Toll
* `[4:1-9]` Moses flags packet rejection again: "They will not believe me." Instead of logistics, rations, or weapons, the controller loads him with three stage-magic overrides: rod into snake (`4:3-4`), hand into snow-white leprosy and back (`4:6-7`), Nile water turning to blood on dry earth (`4:9`). None of these feed a starving brickmaker; all three hack human threat perception to force compliance through shock.
* `[4:10-17]` Moses exposes a real physical defect: `כבד פה וכבד לשון` ("heavy of mouth and heavy of tongue", slow vocal bus, high speech latency). He asks to be replaced (`שלח נא ביד תשלח`). The controller erupts in anger (`ויחר אף`), refuses to swap the chassis or fix the vocal bus, and instead patches the topology with `Aaron` (`4:14-16`): Aaron becomes the low-latency public mouth (`יהיה לך לפה`), while Moses sits behind him in the role of backend god (`ואתה תהיה לו לאלהים`). That single line exposes the priesthood's permanent architecture: silent authority behind a fluent front-end speaker.
* `[4:18-23]` Moses returns to Jethro and lies cleanly (`4:18`), saying he only wants to check if his brothers in Egypt are still alive. Once the old Egyptian arrest warrant clears (`4:19`, "all who sought your life are dead"), he packs his wife, sons, and the rod onto a donkey. Along the road (`4:21-23`), the controller admits a brutal test-rig manipulation: "I will stiffen Pharaoh's heart (`ואני אחזק את לבו`) so he refuses to let them go," setting up the escalating threat to murder Pharaoh's firstborn son. Deliberately locking an adversary's state register so you can justify maximum destructive escalation is pure power politics, not moral justice.
* `[4:24-26]` Unresolved nocturnal rupture: at a roadside camp, the exact same God who just dispatched Moses ambushes him and tries to kill him (`ויפגשהו יהוה ויבקש המיתו`). No explanation is recorded in the text. `Zipporah` acts faster than the prophet: she grabs a sharp flint, cuts off her son's foreskin, touches Moses's legs/genitals with the bloody tissue, and calls him `חתן דמים` ("bridegroom of blood"). The attacker releases Moses. The priests preserved this jagged scar because they could not erase it: the covenant demands fresh blood on flesh before crossing into battle, and a foreign Midianite woman with a stone blade had to pay the toll while Moses was pinned down.
* `[4:27-31]` Aaron intercepts Moses at the mountain, receives the full instruction set and demo scripts, and takes them to the Israelite elders. Aaron speaks, runs the three tricks, and the exhausted crowd collapses into belief and prostration (`ויאמן העם... וישתחוו`). Low-voltage populations buy spectacle fast when current hope is zero.

## Chapter 5: First Contact Blowback and Impossible Thermodynamics
* `[5:1-5]` Moses and Aaron run first contact on Pharaoh, demanding desert leave for a festival. Pharaoh responds with an exact routing-table check (`5:2`): `מי יהוה אשר אשמע בקלו... לא ידעתי את יהוה` ("Who is this deity that I should obey him? He is not registered in my system, and I am not releasing labor"). Pharaoh diagnoses the request strictly as productivity sabotage (`תפריעו את העם ממעשיו`).
* `[5:6-14]` Pharaoh answers political agitation with a direct thermodynamic squeeze. He cuts off straw input (`תבן`) while locking the daily brick quota (`מתכנת הלבנים`) unchanged. The workers must now scatter across Egypt scavenging dry stubble while still hitting full daily production (`כלו מעשיכם דבר יום ביומו`). In physical terms, Pharaoh cuts fuel/binder supply, spikes search-and-transport joules, and demands identical work output. When physics refuses to bend and daily counts fall short, Egyptian taskmasters beat the Israelite foremen (`שטרי בני ישראל`), the middle-layer buffers trapped between management and the mud line.
* `[5:15-21]` The bruised foremen appeal to Pharaoh (`5:15-16`), pointing out the broken math cleanly: no straw issued, full bricks demanded, foremen beaten for the host's own supply cut. Pharaoh cuts them off with pure contempt (`נרפים אתם`, "you are shirkers"). Stepping out from the palace, the foremen catch Moses and Aaron standing idle outside and deliver the sharpest reality check in the entire scroll (`5:21`): `הבאשתם את ריחנו בעיני פרעה... לתת חרב בידם להרגנו` ("You made us stink to Pharaoh and his staff, putting a sword in their hand to kill us"). That is the unpaid invoice of naive insurgency: the middle operators and workers bleed first while the prophet stands clear of the whips.
* `[5:22-23]` Moses absorbs the hit and throws an unvarnished failure report straight back at his sender: `למה הרעתה לעם הזה... והצל לא הצלת את עמך` ("Since I came to speak in your name, he has made things worse for this people, and you have saved nobody"). At `10.99V` with `patience=0.4888`, empty promises and worse beatings break trust immediately.

## Chapter 6:1: The End of Words
* `[6:1]` No apology comes back for the beaten foremen, no explanation for why straw was stripped, and no defense of the failed first meeting. Only one mechanical fact survives: `כי ביד חזקה ישלחם וביד חזקה יגרשם מארצו` ("By a stronger hand he will send them out; by a stronger hand he will drive them from his land"). Words, lineage claims, and three-day festival pretexts change nothing in an entrenched power lattice. Only superior physical force (`ביד חזקה`) shifts the state register.

* **Broken Trust at `10.99V` (`5:20-23`)**: Prophets promise rescue; foremen take the whips; workers hunt stubble in the dirt. Every grand ideological release script hits this wall when actual energy input drops and workload stays locked.
* **The Un-Erased Blood Price (`4:24-26`)**: Before the liberator can cross into Egypt, his own patron tries to terminate him, and only a flint blade in Zipporah's hand settles the debt. Nothing moves off the bench for free.
